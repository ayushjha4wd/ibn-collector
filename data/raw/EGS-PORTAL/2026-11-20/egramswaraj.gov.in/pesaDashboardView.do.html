About PESA

The Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 (PESA) is a landmark law that extends the framework of Part IX of the Constitution (73rd Amendment Act, 1992) to the Fifth Schedule Areas, predominantly inhabited by tribal communities. The PESA Act is based on the recommendations of the Bhuria Committee (1995), constituted under the chairmanship of Shri Dileep Singh Bhuria by the Government of India in 1994. The Committee emphasized that tribal self-governance must be rooted in traditional institutions and not be restricted to the uniform structures of Panchayati Raj. Recognizing the unique socio-cultural practices, customs, and governance traditions of tribal societies, the PESA Act, 1996 was enacted to provide self-rule to Gram Sabhas in Scheduled Areas. It empowers them to safeguard traditions, manage natural resources, and take decisions affecting their community.

PESA applies to Fifth Schedule Areas across 10 States: Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan, and Telangana.

PESA is thus envisioned as a bridge between the constitutional framework of Panchayati Raj and the tribal traditions of self-rule, with the Gram Sabha at the core of participatory democracy in Scheduled Areas.

PESA Act, 1996

The Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 (PESA), was enacted on 24th December 1996, to extend the framework of Part IX of the Constitution (73rd Amendment Act, 1992) to the Fifth Schedule Areas, which are predominantly inhabited by tribal populations. While Article 243M(1) of the Constitution of India excludes the application of the provisions of the Part IX of the Constitution to ‘Scheduled Areas’ referred to in Article 244(1) of the Constitution, Article 243M(4)(b) of the Constitution empowers Parliament to extend, by law, the provisions of Part IX to such Schedule Areas subject to certain exceptions and modifications as may be specified in such law. Accordingly, the Parliament enacted “The Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996”, thereby giving legal recognition to tribal self-governance.

Salient features of the Act include:

  1. Gram Sabha as the cornerstone – every village in the Fifth Scheduled Area must have a Gram Sabha, competent to safeguard and preserve the traditions and customs, their cultural identity, community resources and the customary mode of dispute resolution.
  2. Control over resources – Gram Sabhas are vested with powers to manage minor water bodies, minor forest produce (MFP), minor minerals, and village markets.
  3. Consent-based decision making – Prior approval of the Gram Sabha is mandatory for land acquisition, resettlement, and rehabilitation in Fifth Scheduled Areas.
  4. Social justice functions – Gram Sabha has the authority to regulate money lending, intoxicants, and prevent land alienation.
  5. Alignment with State laws – States are required to amend their Panchayati Raj laws to bring them in conformity with PESA.

The PESA Act, 1996 represents a paradigm shift in governance, by legally empowering tribal communities to govern themselves, protect their culture, and manage their local resources in line with their traditions and customs.
