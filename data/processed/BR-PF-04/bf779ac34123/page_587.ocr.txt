Child Cabinet (Bal Sansad)

Currently, every school in Bihar has established a child cabinet referred to as “Bal Sansad’, These
cabinets consist of a committee of 12 students and have been implemented by the State government
across all primary schools. Their primary responsibility is to support the school management in
promoting greater involvement of children in the educational process. The Bal Sansads aim to
foster democratic values among children by developing leadership skills and enhancing
communication abilities. These child cabinets are an appropriate forum for discussing issues
related to their school, the surrounding community, health and hygiene needs, and their rights as
individuals.

Bal Samvad Adalat (Courts of Dialogue with Children)

Juvenile delinquency denotes the socially unacceptable behaviour exhibited by minors with
criminal inclinations, a phenomenon observed worldwide. Typically, these young offenders
emerge from disadvantaged socio-economic backgrounds and are commonly referred to as CICL,
or ‘Children in Conflict with Law.' The issue gathered attention during the UN Congress in 1960.
Following the tragic events of the 'Nirbhaya Case! in December 2012, the Juvenile Justice Act of
2015 was enacted, subsequently leading to the establishment of the Bihar Juvenile Justice Rules
in 2017. This legislation encompasses comprehensive provisions aimed at the rehabilitation of
these children, emphasizing reformative rather than punitive measures. It also prioritizes the safety,
security, dignity, and overall well-being of the accused minors. Offenders committing minor
infractions are effectively addressed through the 'Bal Samvad Adalat,’ which has been instituted at
the Patna High Court and operates on principles of dialogue and consultation with the children,
facilitated by Juvenile Justice Boards at the district level. This innovative approach is unique to
Bihar, with the first special Child Dialogue Court already operational at the Civil Court in Patna.
Furthermore, a 'Bal Mitra Nyayalaya’ is in the process of being established in all districts across
Bihar.

Meena Manch

A significant initiative introduced by the State government is the establishment of “Meena Manch’
aimed at girls in upper primary classes. This group comprises 20 girls, including 15 from upper
primary schools and five who have cither dropped out or haye only completed education up to
Class V. Within these Meena Manch participants, one girl is chosen as ‘Meena Prerak’.
Additionally, a female teacher from each model cluster school is appointed as *Meena Sugamkarta*
to facilitate the activities of the Meena Manch in their respective schools. The aim is to raise
awareness among girls regarding their rights and responsibilities to foster leadership skills. The
Manch is tasked with ensuring that students are admitted to school in a developmentally
appropriate manner, monitoring their attendance, supporting their completion of elementary
education, and promoting personal growth.
