Scheme for Adolescent Girls (SAG)

The Scheme for Adolescent Girls (SAG) has been revised and subsumed under Mission Saksham
Anganwadi and Poshan from April, 2022. The targeted beneficiaries under this scheme are girls
aged 14-18 years in the Aspirational Districts. The SAG is implemented through 178 projects in
13 aspirational districts of Bihar, These districts are Araria, Aurangabad, Banka, Begusarai, Gaya,
Jamui, Katihar, Khagaria, Muzaffarpur, Nawada, Purnea, Sheikhpura, and Sitamarhi. The scheme
consists of two components, viz., nutrition and non-nutrition, Under the nutrition component,
supplementary nutrition containing 600 calories, 18-20 gm of protein and micronutrients is
provided in the form of Take-Home Ration (THR) for a maximum of 300 days in a year. The
non-nutrition component is based on convergence with different ministries wherein the Iron Folic
Acid tablets supplementation, health check-up and referral services, nutrition and health education,
skill training ete. is provided.

A. Cost for Nutrition ; The financial norm for the nutrition component is Rs. 9.50 per
beneficiary for 300 days in a year. This includes the cost of micronutrient fortification,

B. Funding pattern : The share of the cost of supplementary nutrition between Union and State
(Bihar) is in the ratio of 50:50.

In 2023-24, 1.15 lakh girls got the benefit of the scheme. For 2024-25, a budget allocation of
Rs. 4198.89 lakh was made, out of which Rs. 3149.16 lakh was the central share and Rs. 1049.73
lakh was the state share.

Financing Nutrition

The inadequate nutritional status is recognized as one of the key factors contributing to distorted
health indicators, The issues of underweight, stunting, wasting, and malnutrition have evolved into
multifaceted health and developmental challenges. Consequently, the high rates of malnutrition
continue to pose a significant problem for socicty, particularly for the most disadvantaged and
vulnerable populations. Implementing nutrition-focused programmes is crucial to alleviate the
burden of undernutrition and foster the overall progress in the state. The SDGs for 2030 highlights
the importance of proper nutrition, Therefore, evaluating investments in nutrition serves as a highly
relevant measure for determining the country's future growth. Table 13.9 illustrates the funding
trends of the nutritional component within the ICDS. The overall budget for ICDS in Bihar,
including state initiatives, rose from Rs. 3800.18 crore in 2019-20 to Rs. 4611.53 crore in
2023-24, indicating an increase at the rate of 4.3 percent annually, Likewise, the budget allocated
for nutrition under ICDS grew at an annual rate of 1.3 percent, increasing from Rs. 1791.89 crore
in 2019-20 to Rs. 2064.72 crore in 2023-24.
