Financial flexibility refers to a state’s ability to increase revenue through various sources or by
taking additional borrowing. Key indicators for measuring this flexibility include: the percentage
of capital repayment compared to capital borrowing, the percentage of capital outlay compared to
capital receipts, total tax receipts as a percentage of Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP), state's
own tax receipts as a percentage of GSDP, state’s own non-tax receipts as a percentage of GSDP.
and outstanding debt as a percentage of GSDP.

Vulnerability indicators assess whether a state’s finances are at risk, potentially hindering its
ability to borrow from various lending sources. By analyzing a range of deficit-related metrics,
these indicators provide valuable insights into the potential risks to the state’s financial health.

A. Sustainability

Part A of Table 2.2 presents data on selected indicators used to assess the sustainability of the State
government’s expenditure from 2019-20 to 2023-24.

(i) Balance from Current Revenue (BCR) ;: A positive balance from the current revenue of
the state finances in a year indicates that the State government has surplus funds from its
revenues to meet the development expenditure. The State government in Bihar has
consistently maintained a positive balance from current revenue over the years. The balance
from current revenue was Rs. 38,333 crore in 2022-23 and Rs. 48,235 crore in 2023-24. The

indicator suggests that state finances are presently sustainable.

(ii) Interest Ratio : The interest ratio is calculated by taking the net interest payments and
dividing them by total revenue receipts, excluding interest receipts, grants-in-aid, and
contributions. A sudden and significant increase in the interest ratio indicates a diminished
ability of the State government to service new debt while still covering its revenue
expenditures with its revenue receipts. The ratio has gradually risen from 10.0 percent in
2019-20 to 11.6 percent in 2024-25, suggesting that the State government's finances are
currently sustainable.

(iii) Growth in Outstanding Debt : The State government's debt at the end of the year reflects
its liabilities. A rapid increase in outstanding debt can challenge the sustainability of state
finances. In 2023-24, the state’s outstanding debt rose by 13.4 percent, which is lower than
the 13.9 percent growth observed in 2022-23. Moreover, there has not been any sudden spike
in the annual growth rate in the outstanding debt in recent years, indicating that the State
government's finances are sustainable.
