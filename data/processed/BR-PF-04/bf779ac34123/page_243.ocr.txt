According to the 15th ICLS (International Conference of Labour Statisticians) conceptual
framework, proprietary and partnership enterprises, including informal producers’ cooperatives,
are largely considered informal sector enterprises. Following the same concept, PLFS reported
that, in Bihar, 90.8 percent of male workers and 78.8 percent of female workers were employed in
the informal sector (see Table 5.8). A larger share of female workers was employed in
government/local bodies/public sector enterprises (16.3 percent) compared to their male
counterparts (5.4 percent), However, in absolute terms, the number of female workers in
government/local bodies/public sector enterprises might not be more than the number of male
workers, as we have seen that the size of the female working population was small in Bihar
compared to the size of the male working population in Bihar.

In Table 5.9, we attempt to capture the employment conditions of regular wage/salaried employees
in usual status (pst+ss) engaged in the non-agricultural sector. Four aspects of employment
conditions are considered here: first, workers with no written job contract; second, workers not
eligible for paid leave; third, workers without any specified social security benefits; and fourth,
those not eligible for paid leave, without a written job contract and without any specified social
security benefits. As mentioned earlier, 10.3 percent of male and 5.5 percent of female workers
were regular wage/salaried employees in Bihar. Among male regular wage/salaried employees,
66.8 percent did not have any written job contracts, Furthermore, 56.7 percent of male regular
wage/salaried employees were not eligible for paid leave, without a written job contract and
without any specified social security benefits. The employment conditions of female regular
wage/salaried employees were better than that of male workers in Bihar.
