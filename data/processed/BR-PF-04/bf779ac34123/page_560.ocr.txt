CHAPTER - XIII
CHILD DEVELOPMENT

Abstract

The well-being of children is crucial for any society, as they represent the future human
resource. According to the 2011 Census, there were about 4.98 crore children (0-18 years) in
Bihar. The State accounts for 11 percent of the total child population in India. Almost 90
percent of these children reside in rural areas. It is the responsibility of any state to protect
their rights and achieving this requires a comprehensive integration of policies focused on
their survival, development, protection and participation. The child welfare budgeting was
started in Bihar in 2013-14 to meet these requirements. Since then, there has been a steady
increase in the allocation for children. Between 2016-17 and 2023-24, the total expenditure
on children rose at an annual rate of 19.4 percent. Throughout this period, the per capita
spending on children rose from Rs, 3275 in 2016-17 to Rs. 9131 in 2022-23, more than double,
Approximately, 18 percent of the total state budget is dedicated to child development
expenditure, though this figure varies from year to year. Similarly, the expenditure as a
percentage of the Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP) hovers around 5 percent. The efforts
of the State government in the education sector, health sector and welfare have contributed to
the decline in IMR, malnutrition and other forms of morbidity. The Integrated Child
Development Scheme, Poshan Abhiyan, Mission Vatsalya and other governmental initiatives
are significantly enhancing the growth and welfare of children in Bihar.

Child Development is a major component of human development, The well-being of children is
indispensable for the country’s development, as they constitute the country’s future human
resource, Bihar is home to 49.8 million children (0-18 years), comprising 48 percent of the State's
total population. The Constitution of India accords a special status to children with special
provisions and protections to secure and safeguard their entitlements of ‘those of tender age’.
Biologically, childhood is the span of life from birth to adolescence. The physical, social and
emotional growth of children has direct impact on their overall development. Thus, understanding
the status of children is of immense importance which may be assessed on the basis of four
criteria — (i) Survival, Health and Nutrition, (ii) Education and Development, (iii) Protection, and
(iv) Participation. To ensure that every child develops well, adequate investment in early childhood
is essential. Every child has a basic right to be born in a safe and non-discriminatory environment
and grow through their formative years of life in a healthy and dignified way. Thus, reducing the
level of micronutrient deficiency, and increasing the enrolment and retention rates in schools are
