14.1 Climate Change

Climate change impacts mostly our water (rainfall) and temperature and results in extreme weather
events such as floods and droughts. Drought and flood risks and associated damages are projected
to further increase with every degree of global warming, as stated by the Intergovernmental Panel
on Climate Change (IPCC).

Rainfall Pattern

The economy depends on rainfall. While heavy rainfall causes floods, its scarcity leads to
droughts. Therefore, rainfall and its pattern are critical, as it replenishes rivers and lakes and
provides the impetus for the growth of agriculture, tourism, construction sectors, etc. Table 14.1
presents the annual rainfall pattern during different seasons in the state. The annual rainfall data
reveals fluctuating trends recorded with minimum rainfall (77.0 percent of the annual average)
during 2013, while maximum rainfall was registered (140.7 percent of the annual average) during
2021. As per Chart 14.1, the average annual rainfall in Bihar during 2011-24 was 1004.6 mm.
Nearly 88 percent of rainfall is recorded during the southwest monsoon. The rainfall is unevenly
distributed across the districts, as given in the Appendix (Table A 14.1).
