Krishi Input Subsidy Scheme : In the scenario of crop loss exceeding 33 percent due to floods
and hailstorms, the State government provides financial support under the Krishi Input Subsidy
Scheme. According to the National Disaster Response Fund (NDRF)/State Disaster Response
Fund (SDRF) norms, the rates of compensations are Rs. 17,000 per hectare for irrigated crops,
Rs, 8,500 per hectare for unirrigated crops, and Rs, 22,500 per hectare for perennial crops. In
2023-24, 87.12 lakh farmers were affected due to hailstorms and unseasonal rains. To provide
financial support to these farmers, the State government disbursed Rs. 55.05 crore.

Contingent Crop Scheme : In the event of crop loss, the farmers were provided with seeds for
alternative crops as a compensatory measure. The State government has spent Rs. 18.15 crore in
2023-24 to distribute these seeds. In 2023-24, 2.05 lakh affected farmers availed themselves of the
scheme to obtain alternative seeds for cultivation.

Climate-resilient Agriculture Programme (CRA)

The objectives of the Climate-resilient Agriculture Programme (CRA) are to enhance the resilience
of agriculture, covering crops, livestock, and fisheries to climatic variability and climate change.
The aim will be achieved through the development and application of improved production and
risk management technologies; demonstrating site-specific technology packages on farmers’ fields
to adapt to current climate risks; and building up the capacity of scientists, researchers, and other
stakeholders in climate-resilient agricultural research and its applications. Several interventions
can be made to build soil carbon, control in soil loss due to erosion, and enhance the water-
retaining capacity of soil, all of which build resilience in soil.

The partners in the project are: Borlaug Institute for South Asia (BISA), Pusa; Bihar Agricultural
University (BAU), Sabour; Dr, Rajendra Prasad Central Agricultural University (RPCAU), Pusa;
and ICAR-Research Complex for Eastern Region (ICAR-RCER), Patna. The nature of the
interventions includes: laser land levelling, crop diversification, climate-resilient varieties, direct
seeded rice, zero tillage. raised bed planting, precision input management, intercropping,
community irrigation, potato-based farming systems, crop residue management, carbon credits,
solar harvesting, millets in food system, and empowering rural women.

The Climate-resilient Agriculture Programme is implemented in 30 districts of Bihar for the period
from 2020-21 to 2024-25. The State government has allocated Rs. 238.49 crore for this period.
