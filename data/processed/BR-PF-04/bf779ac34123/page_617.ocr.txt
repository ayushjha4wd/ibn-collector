and restore ecological balance, the Department has launched the Ganga Jal Apurti Yojana.
This initiative is designed to pump plentiful surface water from the river Ganga near
Maranchi village in the Mokama block under the Patna district during the monsoon months
(July to October). The water collected will be directed underground to storage reservoirs
through pipeline, which will later be treated and made accessible for drinking and domestic
needs in the cities of Gaya, Bodhgaya, Rajgir, and Nawada. Additionally, constructing these
storage reservoirs will positively impact the local flora and fauna. By decreasing the demand
for domestic water, it is anticipated that the water flow at nearby locations will be restored
to its normal level. Groundwater replenishment will take place around the Rajgir and Tetar
reservoirs. Furthermore. this programme will provide advantages to the animals and aquatic
life in the Zoo Safari and Nature Safari areas of Rajgir. Administrative approval of
Rs. 4515.70 crore has been accorded for the implementation of this scheme by the Water
Resource Department under the Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali scheme. This scheme has been almost
completed. Presently, drinking water is being supplying to Rajgir, Gaya, Bodhgaya and
Nawada town. Residents and tourists in these towns are benefiting from this scheme.

Under Ganga Jal Apurti Scheme part 11 : drinking water will be supplied to Biharsharif

Drinking Water Scheme for Aurangabad, Dehri, and Sasaram Cities under Jal Jeevan
Hariyali Mission : The work of providing drinking water to the cities of Aurangabad, Dehri,
and Sasaram using surface water available in the Son River at a cost of Rs. 1347.32 crore is
underway under the Jal Jeevan Hariyali Mission. These cities are facing drinking water issues
due to the rapid decline in groundwater levels. Provision has been made for the supply and
distribution of a total of 206 MLD (Million Liters per Day) of water to these cities, which
are important from both tourism and religious perspectives. The scheme has been designed
considering the domestic water demand of the projected population of these cities in 2051,
Under this scheme, the abandoned anicut (weir) built in 1874 is being modernized to store
the required water. With its help, surplus water released from below the Indrapuri Barrage
will be stored and used for drinking water. The scheme is expected to be completed by
December, 2026.
