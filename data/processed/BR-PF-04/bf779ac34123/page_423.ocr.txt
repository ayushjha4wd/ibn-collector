Wastewater Management ; Wastewater management under the Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban)
2.0 is another crucial area. This involves the development of infrastructure like Sewage Treatment
Plants (STPs) and Faecal Sludge Treatment Plants (FSTPs) for cities with populations below one
lakh. In addition, cesspool tankers are being introduced to facilitate emptying of septic tanks,
which helps manage wastewater in areas lacking direct sewer connections.

Publicity and awareness drives/activities are designed to educate and inform the community about
sanitation practices. Various media channels, including mass media, mid-media, digital platforms,
and interpersonal communication, foster behavioural change and enhance public engagement.

Capacity Building : Capacity building is also an integral aspect of the mission. aimed at
equipping sanitation workers, municipal officials (including Junior Engineers and Assistant
Engineers), and women Self-Help Groups (SHGs) with the skills needed for effective waste
management, This training focuses on improving their ability to perform their duties efficiently,
ultimately strengthening the overall sanitation infrastructure and practices in urban areas.

Plastic Waste Management : In response to the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate
Change's gazette notification banning single-use plastics, the Model Bihar Municipal Plastic Waste
Management Bye-laws of 2018 have been revised to the Bihar Municipal Plastic Waste
Management (Revised) Bye-laws of 2022, which all municipal bodies are required to adopt. The
revised bye-laws prohibit the manufacturing, import, storage, transport, sale, and use of plastic
carry bags and single-use plastics, including items such as plastic balloon sticks, cutlery, thermocol
decorations, and plastic wrapping films. Violators will face fines for non-compliance.

Special Environmental Squads have been established in 138 municipal bodies to enforce a ban on
the dumping of plastic in historical, religious, public places as well as along rivers and drains, and
to prohibit the open burning of plastic waste. Additionally, 141 city squads are actively working
