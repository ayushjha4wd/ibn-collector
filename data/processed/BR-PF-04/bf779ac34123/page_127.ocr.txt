Specifically, per capita spending by the State government on Education, Sports, Arts, and Culture

Quality Parameters of Expenditure

An assessment of the quality of expenditure made by the State government could be done using
some of the fiscal parameters. The select quality of expenditure parameters include: Ratio of
capital outlay to total expenditure; Ratio of capital outlay to GSDP; Ratio of revenue expenditure
on Social and Economic Services to GSDP; and Ratio of non-salary expenditure incurred on Social
and Economic Services. The values of these parameters for the period 2019-20 to 2023-24 and the

budget estimate for 2024-25 are presented in Table 2.34.

An increased proportion of capital outlay within government expenditure suggests a prioritisation
of funds to support long-term growth. The pattern of capital outlay as a percentage of total
expenditure and Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP) is illustrated in Chart 2.14. The share of
capital outlay in the State government's total expenditure rose from 8.4 percent in 2019-20 to 14.5
percent in 2023-24. Additionally, capital outlay increased from 2.0 percent of the state's GSDP in
2019-20 to 4,3 percent of GSDP in 2023-24. A significant improvement in these quality measures

of expenditure has been observed over the past five years.

Revenue expenditure on social and economic services has grown from Rs. 84,387 crore in
2019-20 to Rs. 1,64,208 crore in 2023-24, There is a notable increase of 5.5 percentage points in
the ratio of revenue expenditure on social and economic services as a percentage of state’s GSDP
during this period (Chart 2.15), The expenditure as a percentage of GSDP climbed from 13.7
percent in 2019-20 to 22.3 percent in 2023-24, with a substantial jump in 2021-22, reaching 23.6
percent compared to 15.1 percent in 2020-21. Furthermore, the non-salary component of revenue
expenditure on social and economic services rose from 86.4 percent in 2019-20 to 90.5 percent in
2023-24. Conversely, salary expenditure as a percentage of revenue expenditure on social and
economic services consistently decreased from 13,6 percent in 2019-20 to 9.5 percent in 2023-24.
Overall, these patterns illustrate an improvement in the quality of expenditure by the State

government in recent years.
