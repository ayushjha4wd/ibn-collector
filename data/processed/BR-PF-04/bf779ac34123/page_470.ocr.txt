Changing Age Composition

The changes in the age structure are the result of demographic transition, including declines in
mortality and fertility. However, the pace of the transition may vary across the country. Chart 12.5
shows the comparative scenario of changes in the age structure of Bihar and India in various age
groups viz. 0-19 years, 20 to 59 years, and 60 years and above. The share of the young population
of Bihar, i.e. 0-19 years is projected to drop from as high as 49.4 percent in 2011 to 30.1 percent
by 2041, registering a decline of 19.3 percentage points. On the other hand, the share of elderly,
60 years and above is projected to increase from 7.4 percent in 2011 to 11.6 percent by 2041, an
increase of 4.2 percentage points. Bihar’s demographic dividend will peak around 2041, when the
share of working-age. i.e. 20-59 years, population is expected to hit 58.3 percent from 43.2 percent
in 2011. An increasing working-age population means a demographic dividend, which will help
the economy grow. A similar trend is also observed in India, However, the share of the increase
of the elderly population is 7.3 percentage points for India, which is 3.1 percentage points higher
than that of Bihar. The increase in the elderly population relates to the potential social support
requirements and burden on the working population. The district-wise details of the age structure
are given in Table A 12.4 (Appendix).
