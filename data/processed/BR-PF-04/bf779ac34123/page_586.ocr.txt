13.9 Child Participation

Participation is recognized by UNCRC as one of the important human rights, which emphasizes
the right of children to freely express their views, formally or informally, Child participation
involves allowing individuals under the age of 18 to play a role in decisions and take action
regarding issues that impact their lives. This is achieved by empowering children and fostering
positive relationships among children, adults, and communities based on mutual respect and
collaboration at various levels—familial, local, national, and international. From an international
policy standpoint, Article 12 of the Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC) establishes child
participation as a right, stating that "States shall ensure that the child capable of expressing his or
her views has the right to freely express those views in all matters affecting the child." In order to
promote participation in matters that concern children within their families, schools, local
communities, institutions, government policies, and legal processes, necessary measures must be
taken. Currently, both State and Central governments have developed programmes to encourage
children's involvement in the decision-making process to guarantee their active participation in all

Kishori Manch

Drop-out of girl students at the secondary level is a major challenge, especially for children
belonging to educationally backward Minority/SC/ST communities. Apart from this, there are
many social reasons which make it difficult for girl students to stay in school. So, to address this,
in 2022-23, the Kishori Manch Programme was implemented in 1300 Secondary/Higher
Secondary schools of 13 districts viz. Araria, Banka, Bhagalpur, Darbhanga, Gopalganj, Katihar,
Kishanganj, Madhubani, Muzaffarpur, Purnia, Sitamarhi, Siwan and Supaul. The Kishori Manch
forum conducted activities as per the guidelines and Module issued by the state. This forum also
selected an ambassador from among the students, who leads the programme. For the ambassador,
priority was given to those female students, who were hesitant to share their adolescence-related
issues with the Manch and teachers. Presently, 57,600 adolescent girls have joined the Kishori
Manch and are getting benefited from this programme. The programme helped in the development
of social and emotional skills among the adolescent girls and changing of attitude of girl students
about themselves, about others and about the school. The programme helped in relieving of social
and classroom behavior and emotional distress such as anxiety and depression. Furthermore,
