Solar street light scheme: The scheme emphasizes on creating clean and prosperous villages
by providing solar street lights in all villages of the State to ensure better lighting and promote
sustainable energy. A robust maintenance mechanism will also be established to ensure
uninterrupted functionality, In 2023-24, a total allocation of Rs. 392 crore has been approved
for the scheme.

Solid and liquid waste management: Comprehensive arrangements for managing solid and
liquid waste will be implemented at the village level. Regular cleaning of drains and streets
will be conducted at the ward level. Solid waste will be collected from households and

disposed of using modern and appropriate technologies.

Solar water pump system: Under the Mukhyamantri Grameen Peyajal Nishchay Yojana,
the goal is to provide safe drinking water to all rural households through individual tap
connections. Infrastructure such as water tanks, pumps, and distribution networks has already
been developed. Initially managed by the Ward Implementation and Management
Committee (WIMC), the scheme is now overseen by the Public Health Engineering
Department (PHED), The Panchayati Raj Department is responsible for electricity to operate
the pumps. Solar-powered water pump systems are proposed to address the rising costs of
conventional energy sources. These systems offer significant advantages, including reduced
dependence on diesel and electricity, low maintenance costs and long operational life,

ensuring sustainability and cost-effectiveness for rural water supply.

Maintenance of existing infrastructure: Under this programme, infrastructure developed
under Saat Nishchay-!, including household tap water connections, paved lanes and drains,
and household toilets, will be fully maintained and remain operational. For 2023-24, Rs, 200
crore has been allocated to Gram Panchayats for implementation, maintenance and
monitoring of Mukhyamantri Gramin Gali-Nali Pakkikaran Nishchay Yojana and
Mukhyamantri Gramin Peyajal Nishchay Yojana. The progress achieved under the Ghar Tak
Pakki Gali-Nali (Gramin) initiative, as given in Table A9.6, highlights the State
government's efforts to enhance the quality of life in rural areas.

9.6 Shyama Prasad Mukherjee Rurban Mission (SPMRM)

The “Shyama Prasad Mukherjee Rurban Mission” was launched in 2015-16 to develop 300 Rurban

clusters across India, with 11 clusters identified in Bihar. These clusters, located in rural areas, are

experiencing urban-like growth but lack civic facilities. They aim to foster economic and

infrastructural development by creating robust infrastructure, reducing unemployment, and

encouraging investment.
