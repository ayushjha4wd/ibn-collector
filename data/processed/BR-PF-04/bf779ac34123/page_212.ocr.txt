As mentioned earlier, unincorporated non-agricultural establishments have been an important
source of employment for a large section of skilled and semi-skilled workers in both rural and
urban areas. In the following table, we present the annual emoluments of hired workers to assess
their levels of earnings from economic activity. It should be noted that the hired workers can be
differentiated into formal or informal categories, depending on the employment conditions they
agreed to with their employers. The formally hired workers enjoy better employment conditions
along with varying levels of social security benefits. A few observations can be made from the
following table (Table 4.14). First, the annual emoluments of hired workers in urban areas were
higher than the annual emoluments of hired workers in rural areas at the all-India levels. Second,
the annual emoluments of formally hired workers were greater than that of the informally hired
workers, both in urban and rural areas. Notably, the share of formally employed hired workers was
much smaller than the share of informally employed hired workers in the total hired workforce.
This implies that a large section of workers survives on low annual incomes derived from working
in unincorporated non-agricultural establishments and are deprived of any social security benefits
