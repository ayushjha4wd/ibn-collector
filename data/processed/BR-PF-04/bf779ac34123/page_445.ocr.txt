11.2 Deposits, Credits and Credit-Deposit Ratio

Banks are essential financial institutions that mobilize deposits and provide credit for consumption
and investment. However, deposit mobilisation and lending do not occur simultaneously in a
specific area, leading to an inconsistent relationship between the amount of deposits and the credit
extended within the same state, The Credit-Deposit (CD) ratio for a state represents the proportion
of deposits collected within that state that are also lent out within the same area. This ratio indicates
how much of the money that banks have gathered from deposits in a region is being approved as
loans in the same region. Additionally, the CD ratio is one of the indicators of banks' involvement
in the state's economic development. While this ratio is influenced by the level of economic
activity and the capacity of the state to absorb credit, it also depends on the willingness of banks

to extend loans to potential borrowers.
