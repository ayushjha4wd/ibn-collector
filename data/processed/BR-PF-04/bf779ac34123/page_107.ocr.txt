Individual states collect SGST, while IGST applies to taxes on interstate purchases and the supply
of taxable goods and services, including imports. IGST revenue is collected by the Central
government before being distributed to the respective states. CGST is part of the central pool of
tax revenues, a portion of which is shared with the states based on the transfer formula
recommended by the Union Finance Commission.

The receipt by the State government in its revenue account from the different sources for the years
2019-20 to 2023-24, along with the budgeted estimate for 2024-25, is summarised in Table 2.12.
The two major components of receipt in the Revenue account are: State’s Own Revenue and
Receipts from Centre, Reccipt from own sources of the State government includes tax and non-tax
revenue. The receipt from the Central government in the Revenue account includes the State’s
share in the divisible pool of tax revenue collected by the Central government and the Grants-in-
Aid received from the Central government.

In the fiscal year 2023-24, the total revenue receipt of the State government amounted to
Rs. 1,93,347 crore, reflecting a 12.0 percent increase compared to the previous year. Of this total,
27.7 percent came from the State’s own tax and non-tax sources. Revenue from the State's own
tax sources rose by 9.9 percent, increasing from Rs. 44,018 crore in 2022-23 to Rs. 48,361 crore
in 2023-24, Meanwhile, revenue from non-tax sources saw a significant increase of 27.1 percent,
growing from Rs. 4,135 crore in 2022-23 to Rs. 5,257 crore in 2023-24. Additionally, out of the
total revenue receipt of Rs. 1,39,729 crore for 2023-24 from the Central government, Rs. 1,13,604
crore was derived from the State's share of the Central government’s divisible pool of tax revenue,
while Rs, 26,125 crore came in the form of Grants-in-Aid from the Central government.
