Swachh Bharat Mission Urban 2.0

The Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban) 2.0 was launched on October 2, 2019, and will continue until
October 1, 2026, with the goal of making municipal bodies garbage-free. The mission comprises
five crucial components: first, construction of Individual Household Latrines (IHHL), Community
Toilets (CT), Public Toilets (PT), and Aspirational Toilets/Urinals:; second, Solid Waste
Management; third, Used Water Management; fourth, providing Information, Education, and
Communication; and lastly, Capacity Building. In the first phase of the Swachh Bharat Mission
Urban Scheme, 4,23,800 toilets were constructed.

IHHL/Community/Public Toilet : The construction of Individual Household Latrines (IHHL),
Community Toilets, Public Toilets, Aspirational Public Toilets, and Urinals are vital parts of this
mission. These projects aim to ensure that urban populations have access to safe and hygienic
sanitation facilities, essential for public health and dignity.

Solid Waste Management : Under the Solid Waste Management Rules of 2016, various initiatives
are being implemented across all urban bodies. Significant advancements in waste management
and sanitation have been achieved through the Swachh Bharat Mission and related programmes.
Waste segregation at the source is actively practised, with specialized vehicles designated for the
separate collection of dry and wet waste. Material Recovery Facilities (MRFs) and composting
units have been established in several municipal bodies to facilitate waste processing. Public
awareness campaigns engage women from SHGs to promote community participation. Six cities,
including Muzaffarpur and Bodh Gaya, are being developed as Model Towns for comprehensive
waste management. Efforts to remediate legacy waste are ongoing in several cities, and the
“Swachhangini Project” empowers women in slums by providing them with sewer cleaning
training and equipment. All municipal bodies have now achieved Open Defecation-Free (ODF)

status.
