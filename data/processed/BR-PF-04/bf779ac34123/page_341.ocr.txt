CHAPTER - VIII
POWER SECTOR

Abstract

Energy plays a crucial role in driving the economic and social development. Bihar achieved
100 percent household electrification in 2018, and since then, the state has witnessed
significant growth in both the consumer base and electricity demand. The consumer base has
now crossed 2,12 crore, with peak power demand reaching a new record af 8005 Megawatt
(MW) in 2024. In recent years, the per capita consumption of energy in the state has also
increased significantly, from 134 Kwh in 2012-13 to 363 Kwh in 2023-24. Financially, Bihar
has made substantial progress, with the aggregate technical and commercial (AT & C) losses
declining from 45.41 percent in 2012-13 to 19.94 percent in 2023-24, surpassing targets fixed
by the Ministry of Power under the Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS). In the
power generation segment, in line with the Central government's targets, the state is focusing
on increasing the share of renewable energy. It is planned to develop around | Gigawatt (GW)
of renewable power in the coming years. Also, there is continuous infrastructural development
in the power transmission network, The number of grid substations increased from 45 in 2005
to 179 in 2024, while the length of transmission lines expanded from 5000 ckm in 2005 to
20393 ckm in 2024. Finally, the State government schemes like 'Mukhyamantri Krishi Vidyut
Sambandh Yojana’, etc. have been planned to manage the rural load of electricity.

8.1 Overview of Power Sector in Bihar

Energy is a fundamental component for driving economic progress, It plays a vital role in
establishing the infrastructure that supports sustained growth. Electricity is among the most
affordable, eco-friendly, readily available, and easily transportable energy sources on the planet,
with minimal adverse environmental impacts. Consequently, ensuring a sufficient and dependable
supply of electric power has remained a key focus for the government. Since the reorganisation of
the previous Bihar State Electricity Board into five distinct companies in 2012 under Section 131
of the Electricity Act 2003, the energy sector in Bihar has seen significant improvements.

Furthermore, several State and Central governments’ initiatives have successfully strengthened the
power infrastructure, with new projects creating new networks and strengthening the existing
network. The state has improved its control over the distribution network, by leveraging advanced
IT tools and modernising its micro-level monitoring systems. As a result, urban areas in Bihar now
receive a nearly 24-hour power supply, while rural areas get around 22 hours per day on an
average.
