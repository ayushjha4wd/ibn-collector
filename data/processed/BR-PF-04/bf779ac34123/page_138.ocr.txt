3.3 Promotion of Horticulture in Bihar

The growth of the horticultural sector in Bihar has been encouraging. The adoption of various
schemes to promote horticulture encourages farmers to take up fruit and vegetable production in
Bihar. Area-wise, the most important fruit has been mango, followed by the banana in 2023-24.
Out of 371.4 thousand hectares of land under fruit cultivation in Bihar, 164.6 thousand hectares
(44.3 percent) consisted of mango orchards in 2023-24. Between 2022-23 and 2023-24, the growth
of the area under mango was only 1.0 percent, while the total mango production has increased by
9.7 percent. This indicates higher yield of mango in 2023-24 compared to that in 2022-23. In
2023-24, litchi production was 345.1 thousand tonnes. The expansion of the area under litchi has
been continuing in Bihar. Between 2022-23 and 2023-24, the area of litchi orchards has increased
by 6.0 percent, whereas the total production has increased by 11.7 percent (see Table A 3.7).

Another high-value fruit grown in Bihar is makhana, In 2023-24, 27.8 thousand hectares of land
were cultivated with makhana, and the total production was 56.4 thousand tonnes. Bihar accounts
for 85 percent of the total makhana production of India. According to the State government’s
estimate, 50,000 households across ten districts (Darbhanga, Madhubani, Saharsa, Madhepura,
Araria, Purnea, Katihar, Kishanganj, Khagaria, and Supaul) of Bihar are engaged at different nodes
of the value chain of makhana production.

Under the aegis of the Mithilanchal Farmer Producer Organisation, the Government of India has
awarded the Geographical Indication (GI) tag to Mithila makhana. It is well recognised that
makhana production has the potential to become a major export product of Bihar, It is expected
that the total export of makhana can be increased to 200 tonnes and the expected traded value is
about Rs. 600 to 800 crore. Moreover, the fruits produced in Bihar, namely, mango, makhana, and
litchi, have created niche markets outside Bihar. These crops realise high prices owing to their
limited production but high demand.

The State government’s support for horticulture has increased immensely in recent years,
especially for makhana. The Directorate of Horticulture has implemented different schemes to
promote makhana production in Bihar. The Directorate has implemented the Makhana
Development Scheme through three components. First, the State government provides assistance
for high-yielding variety (HY V) seed production to the Bhola Paswan Shastri Agricultural College,
Purnea, and the National Research Centre of Makhana (NRC) at Darbhanga to ensure the supply
of quality seeds for farmers. The state has provided a 100 percent subsidy for HYV seed
production. To demonstrate improved seed, the State government has given a 75 percent subsidy
to makhana farmers. The State government has provided a subsidy of 75 percent on a unit cost of
Rs. 10 lakh to construct makhana storage houses with a capacity of 5 MT. This scheme aims to
promote makhana cultivation, and enhance storage infrastructure for farmers. The State
government has allocated Rs, 1081.97 lakh for the Makhana Development Scheme for 2023-24 to
2024-25.
