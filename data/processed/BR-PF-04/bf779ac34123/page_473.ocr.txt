Health Infrastructure

Health care refers to the delivering services aimed at enhancing the overall health of individuals.
A robust health infrastructure is undoubtedly essential for the provision of such services.
Healthcare infrastructure encompasses a well-balanced combination of physical facilities and
human resources, as both components are necessary to provide the required health services. In
Bihar, healthcare services are organized into three levels: primary, secondary, and tertiary. Primary
healthcare represents the initial point of interaction between individuals and the healthcare system.
This level of care is offered through a network of Sub-Centres (SC), Primary Health Centres
(PHC), and Additional PHCs (APHC). Secondary healthcare functions as a second tier of the
health system, where patients from primary care are referred to specialists in larger hospitals for
treatment. The secondary healthcare services consist of District hospitals, Sub-divisional hospitals,
and Community Health Centres at the block level. Tertiary care indicates a third level of
healthcare, where specialized consultative services are provided, typically following referrals from
primary and secondary care. This level includes specialized intensive care units, advanced
diagnostic support services, and expert medical personnel, Medical colleges and hospitals deliver
the tertiary care services (Chart 12.6).

Most of the population in Bihar relies on public health services for preventive and protective
healthcare. Recently, the State government has implemented several practical measures to enhance
the operations of public health institutions, The recent enhancements in the performance of health
care systems in Bihar are reflected in the average monthly patient visits to government hospitals
(Chart 12.7). In 2023, the number of patients visiting hospitals per month was 10,914, denoting an
increase of 92.01 percent over 5684 in 2020. Moreover, it is remarkable to note that between April
and September 2024, the average number of patients visiting hospitals each month was even higher
at 11705.
