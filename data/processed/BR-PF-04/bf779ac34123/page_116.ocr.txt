The State government incurs expenses for tax collection. An efficient tax system minimizes these
costs while maximizing revenue collection. The costs associated with collecting revenue from
State Goods and Services Tax (SGST) and Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) have been
minimal for State governments, as they do not need to invest significantly in managing the GST
infrastructure. The cost of collecting revenue from two other major tax categories in the state:
vehicle taxes and stamp duty and registration fees, covering the period from 2019-20 to 2023-24
is illustrated in Table 2.22. The percentage cost of collecting revenue from vehicle taxes has risen
from 2.7 percent of total collections in 2019-20 to 4.7 percent in 2023-24. Conversely, the cost of
collecting revenue from stamp duty and registration fees has decreased from 1.9 percent of total

collections in 2019-20 to 1.6 percent in 2023-24.

A comparison of the budget estimates for revenue receipts from both tax and non-tax sources of
the State government for 2023-24, alongside the actual revenue collection for that year, is
presented in Table 2.23. The total revenue from the State government's own tax and non-tax
sources amounted to Rs, 53,618 crore, slightly below the budgeted estimate of Rs. 56,212 crore
for 2023-24. This shortfall was primarily due to lower collections from the State Goods and
Services Tax (SGST), non-tax revenue from non-ferrous mining and metallurgical industries, and
interest receipts that did not meet the estimated targets. However, collections from stamp duties
and registration fees exceeded the target by Rs. 48 crore. Additionally, taxes and duties on

electricity were 61 percent higher than the budgeted target.
