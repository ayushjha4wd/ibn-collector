The summary of the State government’s various components of receipts and expenditures,
expressed as a percentage of Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP) from 2019-20 to 2023-24,
along with the budgeted estimate for 2024-25, is also presented in Table 2.1, The increase in
economic activity has led to the expansion of the GSDP, which grew from Rs, 6.2 lakh crore in
2019-20 to Rs. 8.5 lakh crore in 2023-24.

The State government's commitment to development is evident in the rise of total expenditure.
which increased from 23.7 percent of GSDP in 2019-20 to 29.5 percent in 2023-24. Notably,
capital expenditure significantly increased, rising from 3.3 percent of GSDP in 2019-20 to 7.2
percent in 2023-24. The total capital outlay also increased commendably, rising by 2.3 percentage
points from 2.0 percent of GSDP in 2019-20 to 4.3 percent in 2023-24.

Regarding receipts, the revenue account of the State government increased from 20,1 percent of
GSDP in 2019-20 to 22.6 percent in 2023-24. Tax revenue contributions rose by 3.8 percentage
points, from 15.2 percent of GSDP in 2019-20 to 19.0 percent in 2023-24, while non-tax revenue
receipts remained almost stable at 0.6 percent of GSDP, with the exception of a higher level of 1.0
percent of GSDP in 2020-21. Additionally, receipts in the Capital account increased from 4.7
percent in 2019-20 to 7.1 percent in 2023-24. In 2023-24, Scheme expenditure constituted 11.9
percent of GSDP, while Establishment and Committed expenses accounted for 17.6 percent of
GSDP.

The Revenue account recorded a surplus of 0.3 percent of GSDP in 2023-24, thanks to the State
government's prudent financial management. The primary deficit stood at 2.1 percent of GSDP,
and total borrowing by the State government was 7.0 percent of GSDP, with public debt repayment
at 2.7 percent of GSDP. Consequently, the outstanding debt of the State government was 38.9
percent, which has remained within the limit recommended for Bihar by the Fifteenth Finance
Commission, Overall, the government has achieved fiscally sustainable socio-economic
development at the current level of expenditure, and its financial management has been sufficiently
flexible to mitigate vulnerabilities that could affect its borrowing capacity.
