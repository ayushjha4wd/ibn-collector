5.2 Social Security Measures for Workers of Unorganised Sector

PLFS data indicate that a significant portion of the working population, both male and female, is
either self-employed or engaged in casual labour. Furthermore, it suggests that most regular
wage/salaried employees also work as informal workers, lacking written contracts with their
employers and without social security benefits. These pattems have been observed across all the
states in India, though in varying extents, and Bihar is no exception. Over the years, the
Government of Bihar has implemented several initiatives to provide social security benefits to
various groups of workers. Additionally, the government conducts skill development programmes
aimed at enhancing the employability of different segments of the working population in Bihar. In
recent years, the State government has actively recruited individuals for positions in the public
sector. This large-scale recruitment in the government sector has a multiplier effect on household
consumption among newly hired government employees, contributing to the overall economic
growth of Bihar, In the following sections, we will discuss the measures the State government has
undertaken to provide skills, training and social security benefits to informal sector workers.

Bihar Shatabdi Unorganized Sector Workers and Artisans Social Security Scheme : The
scheme was implemented on April 1, 2011, to provide social security for unorganised workers and
artisans (age group of 18 to 65 years) in the event of death, various disabilities, and injuries
resulting from accidents. It also offers scholarships to the children of victims to support their
pursuit of school, vocational, and technical education. However, the scheme does not cover
individuals registered under the Aam Aadmi Bima Yojana (AABY) or the Building and Other
Construction Workers (Conditions of Service) Act, 1996. This initiative provides financial
assistance to unorganized sector workers and their dependents, including Rs. 2,00,000 for
accidental death, Rs. 50,000 for natural death, Rs. 1,00,000 for permanent total disability, and Rs.
50,000 for permanent partial disability. In 2023-24, the compensation amounts were increased.
Additionally, the number of beneficiaries and the amount of total grant have significantly risen
during this period. In 2023-24, the number of claims reached 2563, representing a 146 percent
increase compared to the previous year, 2022-23. The total grant amounted to Rs. 14.99 crore,
reflecting a 144.1 percent increase from 2022-23 to 2023-24.
