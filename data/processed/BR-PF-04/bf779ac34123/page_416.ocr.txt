Rainwater Harvesting and Artificial Recharge of Ground Water

Rainwater harvesting involves the collection and storage of rainwater for future use, which helps
reduce surface runoff and conserve water. This sustainable method effectively addresses water
scarcity, particularly in regions with irregular rainfall patterns. Common techniques for rainwater
harvesting are rooftop harvesting, soak pits, and storage tanks. Artificial recharge of groundwater
enhances the natural replenishment of aquifers, This method helps mitigate declining groundwater
levels, prevents over-extraction, and supports sustainable water management, particularly in urban
and drought-prone areas. Such practices are essential for ensuring water security and promoting
environmental sustainability,

The Department of Urban Development and Housing has implemented several measures to
enhance groundwater levels in urban areas. Table 10,5 highlights initiatives such as renovation of
wells, construction of soak pits, and installation of rooftop rainwater harvesting systems in
municipality-owned buildings. Among these initiatives, the highest achievement against the target
was recorded for the construction of soak pits near public hand pumps, reaching 84.8 percent. This
was followed by the installation of rooftop rainwater harvesting systems in 76.4 percent of

municipal buildings, and renovation was done in 75.6 percent of the public wells (see Chart 10.3).
