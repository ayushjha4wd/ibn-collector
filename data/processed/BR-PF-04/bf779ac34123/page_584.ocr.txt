13.8 Child Protection

The Constitution of India places the utmost importance on the safety and well-being of children.
Policymakers widely acknowledge that sufficient investment is essential for the survival,
education, protection, and overall welfare of children in order to promote inclusive growth.
Numerous children, in Bihar and beyond, encounter various vulnerabilities in accessing quality
nutrition, healthcare, education, and additional services. Moreover, children frequently face new
challenges such as online exploitation, climate change, and both natural and man-made disasters.
To address these issues concerning the safety and well-being of children, the State Government
has already implemented certain initiatives. India is also a signatory to the United Nations
Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC) and, as such, has a strong legal framework to
protect children, This legal framework includes the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of
Children) Act 2015. The Government of India established the National Commission for Protection
of Child Rights (NCPCR) in accordance with the Commission for Protection of Child Rights
(CPCR) Act of 2005, to carry out the responsibilities and powers granted to it under this Act. Some
of the initiatives taken by the State government to protect the rights of the children are as follows:

Mission Vatsalya (Integrated Child Protection Scheme)

Mission Vatsalya serves as a strategic framework aimed at fulfilling development and child
protection objectives in accordance with the SDG. It underscores the importance of child rights,
advocacy, and awareness, while also enhancing the juvenile justice care and protection system,
guided by the principle of ‘leaving no child behind.’ The foundational legal instruments for
implementing this Mission are the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act of 2015
and the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act of 2012. Before the fiscal year 2009-10,
the Ministry executed three distinct schemes: (i) the Programme for Juvenile Justice for children
requiring care and protection, as well as those in conflict with the law; (ii) the Integrated
Programme for street children; and (iii) the Scheme for assistance to homes for children (Shishu
Grih). These initiatives were subsequently consolidated into a single centrally sponsored initiative
known as the Integrated Child Protection Scheme (ICPS), which was operational from 2009-2010.
In 2017, the ICPS was rebranded as the “Child Protection Services” Scheme, As of the 2021-22
period, the CPS Scheme has been integrated into Mission Vatsalya,

For this scheme, a memorandum of understanding (MOU) has been executed between the
Government of Bihar and the Central government to facilitate the implementation at various levels.
