12.1 Demographic Status

Demographic data are essential for the planning and implementation of state policies, especially
those for economic development and public welfare. Significant changes over the centuries have
marked the history of population growth in Bihar. The state has experienced periods of rapid
population growth and relative stability. The state has witnessed a significant decline in fertility
and mortality and began experiencing a demographic transition where birth rates started to decline,
with better education and healthcare opportunities, a fast pace of economic growth, etc. The first
census was organized around 1872, but it was not synchronal. Since 1881, censuses have been
conducted regularly at intervals of 10 years up to 201 1. However, the planned 2021 census was

delayed due to challenges associated with the Covid-19 pandemic and is now scheduled for 2024.

As per the 2011 census, Bihar's population density stands at 1106 individuals per sq. km, three
times higher than India's density of 382 individuals per sq. km. In terms of sex ratio, Bihar's figure
is lower (918) compared to the national average (943); however, the child sex ratio in Bihar (935)
surpasses the national average (919) (Chart 12.2). Between 2001 and 2011, the population growth
rate in Bihar was 25.1 percent, compared to 17.6 percent for the entire country. The variation in
decadal growth of population in the districts of Bihar and inter-district variations concerning

demographic features are presented in Table A 12.1 and Table A 12.2 (Appendix).
