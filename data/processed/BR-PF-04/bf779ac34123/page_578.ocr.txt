13.7 Educational Programme for Young Children

Education increases the capabilities of individuals and becomes a catalyst for the closely
interrelated economic, social, cultural, and demographic changes that lead to the overall
development of a society, The Indian Education System, according to the National Education
Policy (NEP) 2020, now has a 5+3+3+4 of schooling, which includes early childhood education
under the domain of formal schooling. Elementary and secondary education have already been
discussed in Chapter 12 on Human Development, so the present section will just present the details
of Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE) and other programmes of child development in
Bihar.

Pre-School Children

Early childhood experiences play a vital role in the growth and development of children. In these
formative years, a child's brain undergoes rapid development, social and personal habits are
established swiftly, and a strong foundation is created for the child's comprehensive development
throughout life. To improve the educational outcomes of students in elementary education, it is
crucial that all children have access to high-quality pre-school experiences. Ensuring quality pre-
school education is a key objective of the SDG. Research indicates that more than 85 percent of
brain development occurs by the age of six, underscoring the necessity of providing suitable care
and stimulation during early childhood to foster healthy brain development and overall growth.

Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE)

Investing in ECCE will lead to substantial returns on investment. Children engaged in ECCE
programmes tend to exhibit enhanced cognitive and socio-emotional development, achieve
superior academic performance, and experience improved health outcomes throughout their lives.
