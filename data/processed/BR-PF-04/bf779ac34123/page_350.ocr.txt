8.4 Generation

The major sources of energy are classified into two categories, conventional which is available in
limited quantity and cannot be renewed and non-conventional which is renewable energy, The
details of the two are:

A. Conventional (Thermal Power)

The three power generation units of the State government — Barauni Thermal Power Station
(BTPS), Kanti Bijli Utpadan Nigam Limited (KBUNL) and Nabinagar Power Generating
Company Ltd. (NPGCL) have been transferred to NTPC to reduce the cost of power generation
by utilizing the expertise of NTPC. Further, the construction work at two more power generation
units viz. Nabinagar Thermal Power Project (Stage 1) and Buxar Thermal Power Project are in
progress,

Barauni Thermal Power Station (BTPS): The BTPS has 9 separate units. Five of them have
already run through their working life and are not in service anymore. Presently, Unit No. 7 (110
MW) has been generating power since 2016, Unit No. 8 (250 MW) has been working since March
2020. Unit No. 9 (250 MW) has been operational since November 2021 and Unit No. 6 (110 MW)
has been functioning since June 2022, after Renovation and Modernisation, Units 7 and 8 have
been decommissioned on March 31, 2024,

Kanti Bijlee Utpadan Nigam Limited (KBUNL); The KBUNL was earlier a joint venture
between NTPC and BSPGCL with an equity partnership in ratio of 65:35. The two old units of
110 MW each have run out of their working life and are no more in use now and their PPA with
Bihar has been terminated on September 7, 2021. The construction work of two additional units
(Unit Nos. 3 and 4) of 195 MW each was completed and the same have started producing
electricity.

Nabinagar Plant (Stage 1): The Nabinagar Power Generating Company Ltd. (NPGCL) was
initially a joint venture of NTPC and BSPGCL, having an equity share ratio of 50:50. Later, the
State government has handed over its share to NTPC and now it is fully owned by NTPC. The
Commercial Operation Date (COD) of Unit No. | and 2 of 660 MW each was declared in
September, 2019 and July, 2021, respectively. Thereafter, Unit No. 3 (660 MW) has come under
commercial operation since June, 2022. Further, under Stage II, 3 units of 800 MW each (total
2400 MW) is planned to be installed at Nabinagar. Bihar is expected to get 1500 MW of power
from the new plant.

The availability of power in the state has shown a steady increase over the years. The total tied up
capacity of powerbase in March, 2023 was 9234 MW and has reached 9543 MW in March, 2024,
with an increase of 3.35 percent. The details of the existing generation capacity in Bihar are given
