In this chapter, we discuss the broad pattern of employment and unemployment in Bihar with
reference to other states of India, using the Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) data. In the
subsequent sections, the State government's welfare schemes for informal sector workers,
employment generated in the government sector, and skill development programmes by different

5.1 Status of Employment and Unemployment

The Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) provides data on various parameters of the working-
age population in India. The latest series of PLFS was for the reference year 2023-24. PLFS
provides quarterly results on key employment and unemployment indicators for urban areas as
well as annual estimates of employment and unemployment indicators in both rural and urban
areas. For annual estimates, both usual status (principal status and subsidiary status) and current
weekly status reference timeframes are considered. The usual status of a person is the activity
status over the reference period of the last 365 days preceding the date of the survey. The current
weekly status is the activity status of a person over the reference period of the last seven days
preceding the date of the survey. Notably, the sampling design, concepts, definitions, survey
methodology, and measurements have remained unchanged since 2017, allowing researchers and
policymakers the opportunity to compare the results of different rounds of the survey to devise

long-term policies.

In this section, we will discuss three indicators of employment and unemployment, namely, the
labour force participation rate (LFPR), worker population ratio (WPR), and unemployment rate
(UR) based on the usual (principal plus subsidiary) status concept. LFPR is defined as the number
of employed persons, and the number of unemployed persons in the total population. The
improvement in LFPR suggests an increase in the economically active population in the total
population, In Bihar, LFPR for males was 79.2 percent in rural Bihar and 71.9 percent in urban
Bihar. However, the female LFPR was lower compared to male LFPR. LFPRs for both males and
females in both rural and urban Bihar were lower compared to the all-India average. Between
2022-23 and 2023-24, the female LFPR in both urban and rural areas has improved. For instance,
LFPR for rural women was 24.8 percent in 2022-23, which increased to 33.5 percent in 2023-24.
Similarly, LFPR for urban women was 18.0 percent in 2023-24 compared to 13.8 percent in 2022-
23. The increase in female LFPR indicates an improvement in women’s involvement in economic
activities.
