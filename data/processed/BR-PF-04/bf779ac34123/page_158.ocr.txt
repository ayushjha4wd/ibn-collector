Agricultural Credit

Farmers require both short-term and long-term credit to invest in farming. They use short-term
credit to cover inputs and other out-of-pocket expenditures during a cropping season. They require
long-term credit to invest in capital goods, such as agricultural machinery, and to create
infrastructure like irrigation facilities in the field, and buildings for agricultural purposes. Under
the Kisan Credit Card (KCC) scheme, farmers receive short-term loans, In Bihar, over the years,
the access to the KCC scheme has expanded. For instance, in 2019-20, 3,01,223 farmers had
KCCs, and that number has increased to 4,68,896 in 2022-23. However, a marginal decline in the
number of KCCs was observed in 2023-24, The total disbursement of KCC loans in 2019-20 was

Rs. 3204.51 crore, which has increased to Rs. 7080.07 crore in 2023-24.

Bihar Rajya Fasal Sahayata Yojana

The Bihar Rajya Fasal Sahayata Yojana provides financial assistance to farmers for crop losses
caused by natural calamities. The benefits of the scheme can be availed by Raiyats (self-cultivating
landowners), non-Raiyats (those cultivating leased land), and others who cultivate both owned and
leased land, The level of assistance depends on indemnity and threshold yields, calculated over the
70 percent indemnity level based on the seven-year average yield. The scheme covers the kharif
crops (rice, maize, soybean, potato, brinjal, tomato, cabbage) and rabi crops (wheat, maize, gram,
mustard, pigeon pea, sugarcane, onion, potato, brinjal, tomato, cabbage, chili). Farmers receive
Rs. 7,500 per hectare (maximum of Rs. 15,000 for two hectares) for the yield loss up to 20 percent.
In cases of yield loss exceeding 20 percent, the affected farmers receive Rs. 10,000 per hectare
(maximum of Rs. 20,000 for two hectares). To prevent any leakage, the financial assistance is

directly transferred to the farmers’ bank accounts.
