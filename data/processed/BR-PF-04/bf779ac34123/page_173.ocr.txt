Soil Testing

The soil testing programme aims to ensure healthy soil for crop production by analyzing its
condition and promoting the balanced use of fertilizers based on test results. This approach
enhances crop yields and reduces cost of cultivation.

Soil Testing Programme

Under the Centrally-sponsored Soil Health and Fertility Scheme, a target of 2,00,000 soil samples
was set for collection and analysing in villages of the state. To promote the use of micronutrients,
zine is being distributed across 7,600 hectares, with an allocation of 200 hectares per district.
Additionally, plans include establishing one soil testing laboratory at the block level in Gaya,
Bhagalpur, and Munger districts, along with three quality control laboratories in Saharsa,
Bhagalpur, and Muzaffarpur districts.

For free testing of soil samples from fields, nine district-level soil testing laboratories are
operational across 38 districts in Bihar, along with nine mobile soil testing laboratories (one per
division). Currently, 72 village-level soil testing laboratories are functional. Fee-based soil testing
is also conducted in laboratories run by the agricultural universities and the Krishi Vigyan Kendras.
All district-level laboratories analyze soil samples based on 12 parameters (pH, EC. OC, N, ?, K,
Zn, Cu, Mn, Fe, S, and 8), To ensure transparency, soil sample collection is carried out through
app-based software, where agriculture department staff upload the farmer's complete address and
geolocation details. In 2023-24, the Digital Soil Health Cards were sent directly to farmers' mobile
phones to expedite the process of delivering soil health results.

To ensure quality control of testing. in addition to the Central Soil Testing Laboratory, the soil
testing laboratories of both Agricultural Universities in the state have been designated as referral
laboratories.
