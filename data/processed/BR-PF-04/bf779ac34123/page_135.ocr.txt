The levels of crop production and productivity are determined by various factors. Rice yield has
declined between 2022-23 and 2023-24. As for the area under cultivation, Aghani rice was
predominantly grown in Bihar. In 2023-24, the yield level of Aghani rice has declined significantly
from 4,199 kg per hectare in 2022-23 to 3,182 kg per hectare in 2023-24. However, the variability
in rice yield across districts has declined between 2022-23 and 2023-24, as observed from the
coefficient of variation across districts which decreased from 28 percent in 2022-23 to 22 percent
in 2023-24. Among all the districts of Bihar, the contribution of Rohtas to total rice production
was 9.5 percent, with the highest yield of 4,649 kgs per hectare in the state. Of the total land
cultivated with rice in Bihar, 193.7 thousand hectares (or 6.2 percent) of land was operated in
Rohtas (see Table A 3.2).

In Bihar, the yield of wheat has increased by 8.8 percent between 2022-23 and 2023-24 (see Table
A 3.3). Furthermore, the variation in yields of wheat has been low across the districts. The
coefficient of variation of wheat yield across districts was 18 percent in 2022-23 and that has
further declined to 11 percent in 2023-24, Wheat yield was highest in Begusarai, at 4,012 kgs per

hectare.

In Bihar, the majority of maize cultivation takes place in the rabi season. The yield of maize in
Bihar has been at par with the all-India average. The maize produced in the rabi season yielded
7,541 kg per hectare in 2023-24, which is 9.0 percent less than the yield of maize in 2022-23. The
variability in yield of maize across districts was higher compared to that of rice and wheat. Except
for Rohtas and Aurangabad, maize was cultivated in all the districts. However, the four
districts — Purnea, Araria, Katihar, and Kishanganj — contributed 53 percent of the total maize
production in the state in 2023-24, The yield of maize has been relatively high in these four
districts, along with Sheohar, Supaul, and Madhepura (see Table A 3.4).
