➢ Odisha: There is shortage of staff/manpower in the State. Vacancies of
Supervisory staffs at state headquarter, district level and sub-district level delay the
monitoring and supervisory work to great extent.
➢ Punjab: There is a shortage of Staff especially technical staff at all levels. More
and more technology is being involved in this work and various other softwares, being
used by different departments, are also being interlinked with the eSewa software.
➢ Rajasthan: Difficulties relating to implementation of Act in some cases such as
penalty, number of copies, adoption etc. were observed.
➢ Tamil Nadu: Non-availability of funds for sustaining the registration of births
and deaths through online facility in terms of software and hardware, inspection of
registration units, organizing training programme for CRS functionaries, Publicity on
CRS at State/Districts/Registration Unit levels etc.
➢ Telangana: Lack of Sub registrars and statistical staff in Municipalities. Large
number of vacant posts of Panchayats Secretaries.
➢ Uttarakhand: There is lack of registration information and the
computerization of old civil registration record/work is also not being done properly.
Lack of manpower, training, publicity, remote rural area and a lot of posts are lying
vacant in birth-death registration cadre of Health department are posing difficulties in
effective implementation of RBD Act.
➢ West Bengal: As such, there is no difficulty in the implementation of the Act at
the field level. However, there are 196 registration units functioning without Data entry
operators.
➢ Chandigarh: No major difficulty was faced during the implementation of the
Act in 2023. All hospitals in Chandigarh are complying with the provisions of the
Registration of Births and Deaths Act and are actively using the CRS portal for timely
registration of events.
➢ Puducherry: No difficulties encountered in implementing RBD Act since
compulsory Registration of vital events was prevalent during the French Rule.
