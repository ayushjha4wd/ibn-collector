Medical attention received by Mother at delivery:
3.36 While recording details of every outcome of pregnancy during continuous enumeration
and half yearly survey, the enumerators and supervisors are required to enquire about the type
of medical attention received by the mother at the time of delivery/abortion of the new born.
Statement 39 below gives the percentage distribution of live births recorded in the year 2024 by
type of medical attention received by the mother at the time of delivery for India and bigger
States/UTs separately by rural and urban areas. Looking at the data, we can see that the
majority of live births in India were attended by medical professionals in government hospitals,
accounting for 71.7% of all live births followed by 23.7% of live births in private hospitals.
Only 2.4% of live births were attended by qualified professionals, while 1.8% were attended by
untrained functionaries and others and just 0.4% of live births received no medical attention in
2024. The data also shows that the percentage distribution of live births by type of medical
attention received varies by residence. For example, in India, a higher proportion of live births
in rural areas were attended by medical professionals in government hospitals (76.6%)
compared to urban areas (56.9%). Conversely, a higher proportion of live births in urban areas
were attended by medical professionals in private hospitals (41.0%) compared to rural areas
(18.0%). No medical attention was received by mothers in 0.5% of rural cases and 0.0% of
urban cases.
3.37 The percentage distribution of live births by type of medical attention received varied
across States and UTs. Births attended by medical professionals in government hospitals varies
from 30.4% in Kerala to 95.5% in Jammu and Kashmir. In contrast, medical attention at the
time of delivery were attended by medical professionals in private hospitals vary from 3.9% in
Jammu & Kashmir to 69.6% in Kerala. Charts 71, 72, and 73 depict the percentage distribution
