CHAPTER 3
ESTIMATES OF FERTILITY INDICATORS
SRS has been providing data for estimating various fertility measures since its inception.
The fertility indicators considered in this chapter are Crude Birth Rate, General Fertility Rate, Age-
Specific Marital Fertility Rates, Total Marital Fertility Rates and Gross Reproduction Rate. Apart
from this, the chapter includes Sex Ratio at Birth. To examine the changes in the levels of fertility
during the last decade, the average values of these fertility indicators for the years 2020-22 are
compared with that for 2010-12.
3.2 The Crude Birth Rate (CBR) at all India level had declined from 36.9 in 1971 to 33.9 in
1981, registering a fall of about 8 per cent. In the next three decades ending 1991, 2001 and 2011,
the Crude Birth Rate reached 29.5, 25.4 and 21.8 respectively. In the last decade during 2011-2022,
there was around 12 per cent decline in Crude Birth Rate, from 21.8 in 2011 to 19.1 in 2022. The
CBR has been continued to be higher in rural areas compared to urban areas, though the rural-urban
differential in CBR has narrowed down over these years, from a difference of 8.6 in 1981 to 5.3 in
2022.
3.3 The Total Fertility Rate (TFR) has declined from 5.2 to 4.5 during 1971 to 1981 and from
3.6 to 2.0 during 1991 to 2022. The TFR in rural areas has declined from 5.4 to 2.2 from 1971 to
2022 whereas the corresponding decline in urban areas has been from 4.1 to 1.6 during the same
period. In 2022, around 94.3 percent of the deliveries were institutional which includes Government
as well as private hospitals. The percentage of institutional deliveries in urban areas was 97.0 as
against about 93.5 percent recorded in rural areas. The data on most of the fertility indicators have
been presented by age, sex and residence for all bigger States/UTs and India. This chapter also
includes data on fertility by level of education, order of birth, birth interval and medical attention at
the time of birth.
3.4 Apart from the fertility indicators at State and National levels, the SRS report 2022 also
provides estimates of birth rates at Sub-State, viz. NSS Natural Division Level. NSS Natural
Divisions have been formed taking into consideration the geography of the State and by grouping
contiguous districts having similar topography, population density, cropping pattern and rainfall
etc. Table 11 of this report contains data on the birth rate besides Death and Infant Mortality Rate
for 71 Natural Divisions of 22 bigger States/UTs.
3.5 The following Statement 14 provides the Crude Birth Rate (CBR) by residence for India and
States/UTs for the year 2022. Although the birth rate has been estimated for all the States/UTs of
India, the other fertility indicators are presented only for Bigger States/UTs in the report. The CBR
for India was 19.1 in 2022, with a higher rate in rural areas (20.8) compared to urban areas (15.5).
Among the Bigger States/UTs, Bihar had the highest CBR of 26.9, followed by Uttar Pradesh (24.2)
and Rajasthan (23.8). The lowest CBR in this group was in Tamil Nadu (12.1) followed by Kerala
(12.4) and Delhi (13.3).
3.6 Among the Smaller States, Meghalaya had the highest CBR of 23.0, whereas the lowest
CBR in this group was in Goa (10.9). Among the Smaller UTs, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman
and Diu had the highest CBR of 16.6 and lowest CBR in this group was in A & N Islands (10.2).
