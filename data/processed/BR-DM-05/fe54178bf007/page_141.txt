Medical attention at delivery
3.36 While recording details of every outcome of pregnancy during continuous
enumeration and half yearly survey, the enumerators and supervisors are required to enquire
about the type of medical attention received by the mother at the time of delivery/abortion
of the new born. From 2004 onwards i.e. including the new sample in 2015, the options on
types of medical attention received by the mother at delivery have been modified to capture
the deliveries specifically at private hospital/nursing homes. The new options include
‘Government Hospital’, ‘Private Hospital’, ‘Qualified professional’, ‘Untrained functionary
and others’ in comparison to ‘Institutional’; ‘Doctor, Nurse or trained midwife’; ‘Traditional
birth attendants’; and ‘relatives or others’ adopted till 1991 SRS sample. Statement 39
below gives the percentage distribution of live births recorded in the year 2015 by type of
medical attention received by the mother at the time of delivery for India and bigger
States/UTs separately by rural and urban areas. At the National level, 52.6 per cent births
were attended by Government Hospitals and vary from 51.5 per cent in rural areas to 55.5
per cent in urban areas. Among the bigger States/UTs, it varies from 37.3 per cent in
Jharkhand to 69.8 per cent in Rajasthan. About 26.7 per cent of birth occurred at Private
Hospital. Medical attention by qualified professionals constitutes 10.9 per cent of total
delivery whereas untrained and others constitute 9.8 per cent. More than three fourth of
deliveries are occurring in institutions and conducted by the qualified professional.
