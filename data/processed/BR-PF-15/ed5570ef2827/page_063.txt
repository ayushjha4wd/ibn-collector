Cold Wave
Cold waves constitute a significant seasonal hazard in India, predominantly impacting the northern
plains, central regions, and parts of northwestern India during the winter months. These events are
characterised by a sudden and sustained drop in minimum temperatures, posing heightened risks to
human health – particularly among vulnerable populations such as the elderly, children, and outdoor
workers
Cold wave events often coincide with dense fog conditions, compounding risks related to public health,
transportation, and economic productivity. There is emerging evidence that climate variability has
rendered cold wave patterns more erratic in recent years, with unpredictable onset and duration.
For the purpose of cold wave hazard assessment, the total number of disastrous cold wave days annually
was considered, sourced from the IMD Atlas for the period 1969–20197.
As per the definition by the IMD, a Cold Wave is declared based on the following criteria.
• The minimum temperature must reach (cid:148) 10°C or less in the plains, (cid:148) (cid:20)(cid:24) °C for coastal areas,
and 0°C or less in hilly regions.
• In addition, the departure from normal temperature (1981–2010 climatology) should be:
o - 4.5°C to - 6.4°C for a cold wave day
o (cid:148) - 6.4°C for a severe cold wave day
If the above criteria are met at least at two stations in a meteorological sub-division for at least two
consecutive days, a cold wave or severe cold wave is officially declared on the second day.
Cloudburst
A cloudburst is a sudden, intense rainstorm that results in more than 10 cm of rain in less than an hour
over a small area (approximately 10 km²). A hailstorm and thunder can also accompany it. Cloudbursts
are common in mountainous areas, especially in the Himalayas. Due to their localised nature, cloudbursts
are hard to predict or detect but can unleash sudden, devastating rainfall, leading to flash floods and
landslides.
The frequency of cloud burst events has been sourced from the IMD for the period 1982 -2023.
