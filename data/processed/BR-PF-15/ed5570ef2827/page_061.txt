Drought
Drought remains one of the most pervasive and recurrent natural hazards in India, exerting far-reaching
impacts on agriculture, water availability, rural livelihoods and food security. With nearly half of the
country’s net sown area being rainfed, droughts pose a significant risk to agricultural productivity and
rural incomes and can have cascading effects on broader economic growth.
Multiple interrelated factors, including deficient seasonal rainfall, delayed onset or early withdrawal of
the southwest monsoon, and erratic intra-seasonal rainfall distribution, typically trigger drought events
in India.
To assess drought hazard across States, data on the area affected by drought (in hectares) during both
kharif and rabi cropping seasons was sourced from the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers’ Welfare.
The dataset spans the period from 2011 to 2024. And this metric serves as a reliable indicator of spatial
and seasonal variability in drought exposure and provides a basis for comparative assessment of drought
risk across States.
Cyclone
Cyclonic storms in India primarily originate over the Bay of Bengal and the Arabian Sea, impacting both
the eastern and western coastlines. Given India’s extensive and densely populated coastline, the risks
posed by tropical cyclones remain substantial.
To quantify cyclone-related hazards, the return period (in years) for cyclonic storms passing annually
within a 50 nautical mile radius of a given location was used. The return period refers to the expected
frequency with which cyclonic storms of a particular intensity are likely to occur within a defined buffer
zone. This data was obtained from the IMD which provides a probabilistic measure of cyclone exposure
across States, and the data pertains to the period 1961-20203.
Landslide
Landslides constitute a major geo-hazard in India, with recurrent occurrences across the Himalayan
region, parts of the Western Ghats, and the Northeastern States. A combination of intense rainfall,
seismic activity, and anthropogenic factors such as deforestation, slope destabilisation and unregulated
construction on fragile terrains typically triggers these events.
