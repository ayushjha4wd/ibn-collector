For the purpose of landslide hazard assessment, the National Landslide Susceptibility Mapping data from
the Geological Survey of India4 was used. This classification identifies areas as ‘Moderately Affected’
or ‘Highly Affected’ based on terrain stability, recorded events, and geophysical characteristics. The
dataset spans the period 2016-2023 and enables State-level comparative assessment of landslide-prone
zones for risk analysis and planning.
Earthquake
Although earthquakes occur less frequently compared to other natural hazards, they pose a substantial
risk in India due to their potential to cause catastrophic damage and large-scale loss of life. A significant
portion of the Indian subcontinent falls within moderate to high seismic zones, particularly the
Himalayan belt, the Indo-Gangetic Plain, parts of Gujarat and Maharashtra, and the Northeastern States.
India’s seismic vulnerability arises primarily from the tectonic interaction between the Indian Plate and
the Eurasian Plate. The persistent northward movement of the Indian Plate results in the accumulation
of stress along geological fault lines, which is periodically released as seismic activity. This geodynamic
process underpins the elevated seismic risk in several regions of the country.
The risks associated with earthquakes are further compounded by rapid urbanisation and inadequate
enforcement of earthquake-resistant building norms, particularly in densely populated and infrastructure-
intensive urban centres. These factors significantly increase the exposure and vulnerability of people and
physical assets to seismic events.
To assess earthquake hazard across States, the Seismic Zonation Map prepared by the Bureau of Indian
Standards for the year 2024 was used and the data related to the number of earthquake events between
2013 and 2023 as obtained from the National Centre of Seismology (NCoS)5. The zonation map
classifies regions based on the expected intensity of seismic activity and provides a consistent basis for
evaluating seismic risk at the national scale. The data from NCoS provides the historical number of
earthquake events for a State along with the magnitude. The data on both these parameters are assigned
a weightage of 2 per cent, 4 per cent, 6 per cent, 8 per cent and 10 per cent based on levels of seismic
zonation and magnitude, respectively. These are then averaged to arrive at the earthquake score.
Hailstorm
Hailstorms, while localised in nature, pose a significant hazard to agriculture, infrastructure, and
property, particularly in the northern and central regions of India. Crops such as wheat, mustard, and
various horticultural produce are especially vulnerable to hail-induced damage, often resulting in
considerable economic losses for farming communities.
A hailstorm is a severe convective weather event characterised by the formation and descent of solid ice
particles-known as hailstones-from cumulonimbus clouds. Scientifically, hailstones are defined as
precipitation particles with a diameter of at least 5 millimetres. Their size and impact potential are
determined by the intensity of the storm, prevailing atmospheric instability, and the presence of wind
shear. Hailstorms typically occur in regions experiencing strong thermal gradients and high moisture
availability and are commonly associated with severe thunderstorms.
