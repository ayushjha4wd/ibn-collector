Heatwave refers to prolonged periods of abnormally high temperatures, often accompanied by elevated
humidity, lasting for several consecutive days. With rising global temperatures, heatwaves have emerged
as one of the deadliest weather-related hazards in India. The impacts are highly State-specific, with
central, northern, and parts of western India experiencing recurrent and severe heatwave events.
For the purpose of heatwave hazard assessment, the total number of disastrous heatwave days annually
was used, as reported in the IMD Atlas for the period 1969-2019.8

Lightning, meanwhile, has become another major and growing hazard in India, accounting for one of the
highest annual fatality counts among natural hazards. It is primarily associated with convective
thunderstorms, which are most frequent during the pre-monsoon (March-May) and monsoon (June-
September) seasons.
For lightning hazard assessment, the average number of lightning flashes was used, based on
observations from the IMD for the period 2014-2023. The values of both these hazards (Heatwave and
Lightning) are averaged to arrive at the State specific disaster score.
Exposure
To estimate exposure to disaster risks, the total projected population for October 2026 was used, sourced
from the Technical Group of Population Projections 2020 constituted by the Ministry of Health & Family
Welfare9. These projections are based on data from the 2011 Census and the Sample Registration System,
which provide long-term trends on fertility and mortality. Population projections offer a forward-looking
estimate of demographic scenarios, which is critical for assessing potential human exposure to hazard
events.

one way or the other. The population inter se is calculated for all the States. However, smaller States
